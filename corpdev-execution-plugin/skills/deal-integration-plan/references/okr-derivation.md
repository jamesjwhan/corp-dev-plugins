# Reference — OKR Derivation from the Deal Thesis

The integration's OKRs are not generic. They come from **why the deal was done.** Read the Deal Memo's
rationale and key assertions, and convert each into a measurable outcome.

## The translation move

For each thesis assertion, ask: *"If this deal worked, what would be measurably true in 6 months?"*
That answer is the objective; the metrics that confirm it are the key results.

| Thesis says… | Objective (outcome) | Example key results |
|---|---|---|
| "Accelerates our payments roadmap 12 months" | Acquired capability live in Toast product | Shipped to GA by M6; integrated into N flows; X customers using |
| "World-class ML team we couldn't hire" | Acquired team retained and productive at Toast | ≥90% key-employee retention at M6; team shipping on Toast roadmap by M3 |
| "130% NRR product we can cross-sell" | Cross-sell motion live and converting | Live to N Toast customers by M6; $X attached ARR; attach rate Y% |
| "Defensive — keep it from a competitor" | Capability secured and roadmap-protected | Integrated/contained by M4; key IP/talent retained |
| "Cost synergy in infra" | Synergies realized | $X run-rate savings by M6; systems consolidated |

## Rules
- **3–5 objectives max.** More than that and nothing is the priority.
- **Every objective ties to an assertion.** If an OKR doesn't trace to the thesis, cut it.
- **Retention is always an OKR** when people are part of the thesis — make acquired-team retention an
  explicit KR (e.g., key-employee retention % at M3/M6), not a hope.
- **Time-bound to 2/4/6 months** to match the milestone cadence.
- **Owned** — each KR has the Roadmap DRI or Engagement DRI on it.

## Anti-patterns
- "Complete the integration" (activity, not outcome)
- "Integrate the team" (vague; what's the measurable end-state?)
- 12 objectives (no priority)
- OKRs with no link to why the deal was done (drift)
