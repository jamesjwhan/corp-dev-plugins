## DASH — DoorDash
 
**Business model**: Two-sided marketplace (consumers ↔ restaurants/merchants). Revenue = take on GOV + ads/services. Deliveroo acquired 2025 — now a meaningful portion of reported GOV but carries structurally lower take rates than legacy DoorDash; always separate organic vs. reported growth.
 
**Primary volume metric**: **Marketplace GOV** (Gross Order Value) — total dollar value of orders placed. This is the single most important number; everything else flows from it.
 
**Causal chain**:
`Orders × AOV → GOV × Take Rate → Marketplace Revenue → Contribution Profit → Adj. EBITDA`
 
**Key metrics to populate scorecard**:
 
| Metric | What it tells you |
|--------|-------------------|
| Marketplace GOV ($B) — **reported** | Total volume including Deliveroo |
| Marketplace GOV — **ex-Deliveroo / organic** | True apples-to-apples growth; management discloses pts of contribution from Deliveroo — back it out |
| U.S. GOV ($B) | Domestic core health; should be separated from international |
| International GOV ex-Deliveroo ($B) | Wolt + pre-acquisition international organic growth |
| Deliveroo GOV ($B, % contribution to total growth) | Acquisition overlay — quantify how much of YoY growth it explains |
| Order count (millions) — reported and organic | Frequency / consumer engagement |
| Average Order Value (GOV / orders) | Mix shift — higher = more grocery/retail; lower = more convenience |
| Monthly Active Users (MAU / MAUD) | Consumer base size |
| Take rate (revenue / GOV %) | Blended rate; watch Deliveroo dilution — Deliveroo runs structurally lower take |
| U.S. take rate vs. international take rate | If disclosed; the divergence explains most take-rate noise |
| Contribution Profit ($M) and per order ($) | Unit economics — most critical margin metric |
| Ads & Platform revenue ($M, % of GOV) | High-margin revenue; ad load expansion is a key monetization lever |
| New verticals (grocery, convenience, non-food) % of GOV | Diversification away from restaurant |
 
**Restaurant software & merchant tools — track separately**:
 
DoorDash has been expanding into restaurant operating software beyond just delivery. These metrics are early-stage but are the forward ARR story for the platform:
 
| Metric | What it tells you |
|--------|-------------------|
| DoorDash Reservations / SevenRooms cities live | Dine-in software expansion; currently in 4+ U.S. cities — watch city count and QoQ adds |
| Reservations covers seated or reservations volume | Engagement with the dine-in product |
| DashPass membership + active merchants | Merchant engagement with subscription infrastructure |
| Merchant software / SaaS attach (% of restaurant merchants using ≥1 software product) | Platform stickiness signal |
| Key enterprise restaurant logo wins | Named accounts (chain restaurants, QSR) signal enterprise go-to-market maturity |
| Storefront / white-label ordering revenue ($M) | Direct online ordering that bypasses the marketplace fee |
 
Note: DoorDash does not yet break out restaurant software revenue as a separate line. Watch for management commentary on merchant tools adoption, Reservations expansion cities, and any early monetization disclosure. These are leading indicators of an eventual SaaS attach layer that could re-rate the stock.
 
**Inflection signals to watch**:
- Organic ex-Deliveroo GOV growth rate — the number to anchor on. If organic decelerates while reported accelerates, Deliveroo is masking slowdown
- Take rate compression driven by Deliveroo mix is expected and structural — the question is whether legacy DoorDash take rate holds or expands
- Contribution margin per order expanding = operating leverage; watch absolute $ not just % of GOV
- Reservations expansion beyond 4 cities = software monetization becoming real
- Ads & Platform growing faster than GOV = high-margin monetization compounding
- Any new metric disclosures around merchant software = signal that a separate segment is coming
**Competitive context**: UberEats (delivery), Instacart (grocery), OpenTable / Resy (reservations), Toast / Square / Olo (restaurant software). DoorDash entering reservations and merchant software puts it in direct competition with Toast's broader platform ambitions.
 
**What "beat" really means**: A GOV beat on organic growth with stable take rate. A beat driven entirely by Deliveroo GOV consolidation at lower take rates is structurally dilutive. Contribution margin per order improvement is the cleanest signal of business quality.

## SHOP — Shopify
 
**Business model**: Commerce platform for merchants. Revenue = subscription fees (fixed per merchant) + merchant solutions (variable % of GMV processed, payments, capital, etc.). GMV spans online, offline (POS), B2B/wholesale, and cross-border — each with different economics.
 
**Primary volume metric**: **GMV** (Gross Merchandise Volume) — total dollar value of goods sold through Shopify. Everything monetizable scales from GMV.
 
**Causal chain**:
`Merchant count × avg store GMV → Total GMV × attach rate → Merchant Solutions Revenue`
`Merchant count × plan price → Subscription Solutions Revenue`
 
**Key metrics to populate scorecard**:
 
| Metric | What it tells you |
|--------|-------------------|
| GMV ($B) — **total** | Aggregate volume; primary growth driver |
| GMV by channel: Online vs. Offline (POS) | POS GMV growth signals brick-and-mortar expansion; different attach rate profile |
| B2B / wholesale GMV (Shopify B2B) | Enterprise wholesale is early-stage but higher-ARPU; watch for management disclosure |
| Shop Pay GMV ($B, % of total GMV) | Not just Shopify-hosted — includes off-platform; growing faster than total GMV = distribution moat |
| International GMV ($B, %) | Geo expansion; Europe and APAC are primary growth vectors |
| Merchant Solutions revenue ($M) | Variable, GMV-linked; higher growth = attach improving |
| Subscription Solutions revenue ($M) | Sticky base; MRR is the underlying signal |
| Revenue / GMV attach rate (%) | Blended take rate; should trend up as services penetrate |
| Shopify Payments GPV ($B) | Payments penetration = GPV / GMV; target 67%+ after recent expansion |
| Shopify Plus merchant count (or % of MRR) | Enterprise mix; higher ARPU, stickier, anchor accounts |
| MRR ($M) and blended ARPU (MRR / merchant count) | Installed base quality; ARPU expansion = upsell working |
| Shopify Capital deployed ($M) | Embedded finance penetration; sticky merchants; balance sheet risk metric |
| Shopify Markets (cross-border GMV, %) | International commerce enablement layer |
| Free Cash Flow ($M, % margin) | Shopify's primary profitability commitment |
 
**Vertical and segment breakdown — track separately**:
 
Shopify does not break out GMV by vertical (fashion, food, electronics, etc.) in financial filings, but management commentary often surfaces segment signals. Key things to track:
 
| Segment | What to look for |
|---------|-----------------|
| **Offline / POS** | POS GMV growth rate vs. online GMV — POS outgrowing online = successful brick-and-mortar push; new POS hardware releases signal investment in this channel |
| **Shopify B2B** | Wholesale/B2B order volume or merchant count commentary; B2B was launched as a Plus feature in 2022 and is growing — any disclosure of B2B GMV or merchant count is a positive signal |
| **Shopify Plus (enterprise)** | Plus is ~30% of MRR at a ~5-10x ARPU premium to SMB plans; Plus merchant count acceleration = moving up-market successfully; key logo wins in Plus matter more than SMB adds |
| **Shop Pay off-platform** | Shop Pay processing GMV beyond Shopify-hosted stores (e.g., Facebook/Instagram checkout, Buy with Prime alternative) — if growing faster than total GPV, it means Shopify is becoming a payments network independent of the storefront |
| **International** | Track which geographies management calls out — Europe, APAC, LATAM each have different competitive dynamics and FX exposure |
 
**Key logo wins to track**: Enterprise brand migrations to Shopify Plus (from Salesforce Commerce Cloud, SAP Hybris, Magento) are thesis-validating. Any named enterprise wins — Mattel, Gymshark, Allbirds, major DTC brands — signal platform credibility in upper-mid market.
 
**Inflection signals to watch**:
- GMV growth reaccelerating toward / above category growth = share gains
- Merchant Solutions growing faster than GMV = attach rate improving (highest quality signal)
- B2B GMV commentary emerging or quantified = early indicator of a new high-ARPU growth leg
- POS GMV share of total rising = offline expansion working
- Shop Pay off-platform GMV growing faster than on-platform = network effect emerging
- MRR ARPU expanding = pricing power or plan mix improving (watch divergence from merchant count growth)
- Capital deployed growing faster than GMV = financial services becoming a meaningful revenue layer
- Management stopping a metric disclosure = yellow flag (happened with merchant count in 2022)
**Competitive context**: BigCommerce, WooCommerce (online); Square/Toast (POS/offline); Salesforce Commerce Cloud, SAP (enterprise); Amazon (merchant services/Buy with Prime). The most important competitive signal is whether Shopify is winning enterprise migrations away from legacy platforms — each win is high-ARPU, sticky, and validates the upmarket thesis.
 
**What "beat" really means**: Merchant Solutions growing faster than GMV (attachment improving), not just GMV acceleration. A B2B GMV disclosure or POS acceleration on top of that is a tier-1 beat. GMV beat driven entirely by same-store sales growth at existing merchants (with no new logos or ARPU expansion) is the weakest form of beat.

## FOUR — Shift4 Payments
 
**Business model**: Full-stack payment processor — integrates POS hardware/software (especially SkyTab/Shift4 Dine for restaurants, Stadium for hospitality/sports) with payment processing. Revenue = spread on end-to-end volume. Restaurant vertical is the volume engine; hospitality/sports/entertainment is the higher-spend premium vertical.
 
**Primary volume metric**: **End-to-end payment volume** (E2E volume, $B) — the subset of total volume where Shift4 provides the full stack (their hardware + their processing). Higher-margin than gateway-only volume.
 
**Causal chain**:
`E2E volume × spread → Gross Revenue less Network Fees (GRLNF) → Adj. EBITDA`
 
**Key metrics to populate scorecard**:
 
| Metric | What it tells you |
|--------|-------------------|
| End-to-end payment volume ($B) | Core growth metric — all analysis anchors here |
| Total payment volume ($B) | Includes gateway-only; E2E % of total tells you mix shift |
| E2E as % of total volume (%) | Mix shift to higher-margin full-stack; target 60%+ |
| Gross Revenue less Network Fees ($M) | Net revenue proxy; more meaningful than gross revenue |
| GRLNF margin (bps of E2E volume) | Spread / take rate; Global Blue acquisition structurally lifts this |
| SkyTab / Shift4 Dine **net new restaurant locations** | Most important restaurant vertical leading indicator; management discloses cumulative or quarterly net adds |
| SkyTab total restaurant locations | Installed base size in restaurant vertical |
| Restaurant ARPU ($ per location per month) | SaaS + payments blended; should expand as upsell modules attach |
| Restaurant vertical volume ($B or %) | How much of E2E volume is restaurant-driven |
| Sports / entertainment / hospitality volume ($B or %) | High-spend locations; stadiums, arenas, casinos, hotels |
| Hospitality ARPU or revenue per property | If disclosed; hospitality transactions are higher-ticket |
| International volume ($B, %) | Expansion thesis (Worldline/Bambora, Global Blue) |
| Key enterprise logo wins | Named restaurant chains, stadium/venue wins, hotel brands |
| Adj. EBITDA ($M, margin %) | Profitability; mid-40s% margin is the target |
 
**Restaurant vertical — track specifically**:
 
Shift4's restaurant segment (SkyTab, now rebranded Shift4 Dine) competes directly with Toast. Track:
- **Net new restaurant locations per quarter** — the single most comparable metric to Toast's net-new location adds. If SkyTab net adds are slowing while Toast accelerates, share is shifting.
- **SkyTab total installed base** — management has disclosed ~40%+ of merchant base is SkyTab; watch for absolute location count disclosure.
- **Restaurant ARPU** — Shift4 has a higher hospitality/enterprise mix than Toast, so ARPU should theoretically be higher than Toast's ~$15-18K ARR per location. Any disclosure that benchmarks this is valuable.
- **Key logo wins** — named enterprise restaurant chain wins (e.g., Dine Brands, casual dining groups, QSR franchisees) validate the move upmarket in restaurants.
**Inflection signals to watch**:
- E2E volume growing faster than total volume = full-stack mix shift accelerating
- GRLNF growing faster than E2E volume = take rate expansion; Global Blue DCC/tax-free layer is the structural driver
- SkyTab / Shift4 Dine net installs accelerating = restaurant volume pipeline growing; decelerating = Toast taking share
- Hospitality/sports volume growing disproportionately = higher-spend vertical penetrating
- International E2E volume crossing 15%+ = diversification becoming real
- Management pulling back on SkyTab location disclosures = possible share loss signal
**Key enterprise restaurant logos to watch**: Dine Brands (Applebee's, IHOP), large franchise groups, casino/resort hotel F&B, QSR chains. Any named win in a restaurant chain with 100+ locations is particularly meaningful.
 
**Competitive context**: Toast (SMB/mid-market restaurants), Oracle MICROS (enterprise hospitality/restaurants), Heartland, Stripe (adjacent). Shift4's edge in restaurants is complex/enterprise environments and hospitality-adjacent venues; Toast owns the SMB/indie restaurant stack.

## NCR Voyix — VYX (Digital Commerce / POS)
 
**Business model**: After NCR split in 2023, VYX is the digital commerce + POS software platform serving restaurants and retail (digital banking segment was divested 2024). Transitioning from hardware-heavy to software/SaaS ARR model via the Voyix Commerce Platform (VCP).
 
**Primary volume metric**: **ARR** (Annual Recurring Revenue) — the key transformation metric. Also watch the Remaining Contract Value (RCV) of VCP applications as the forward ARR signal.
 
**Causal chain**:
`Net-new registered sites × software ARPU → ARR → Recurring revenue % → EBITDA expansion`
 
**Key metrics to populate scorecard**:
 
| Metric | What it tells you |
|--------|-------------------|
| ARR ($M, or RCV as proxy) | SaaS transformation progress; RCV = $293M +75% YoY in Q1 2026 — use as forward ARR signal |
| Recurring revenue % of total | Mix shift away from hardware; target 75%+ |
| **Restaurant segment: registered locations** | Net installed restaurant base; this is the direct comparable to TOST and FOUR |
| **Restaurant segment: ARPU per location** | Monetization efficiency vs. Toast ($~15-18K ARR/location) and Shift4 |
| Restaurant segment revenue ($M, YoY) | Watch for stabilization or return to growth |
| Retail segment: registered sites | Larger install base than restaurant |
| Retail ARPU per site ($) | Monetization of retail POS / self-checkout |
| Retail segment revenue ($M, YoY) | Larger base, slower growth; should be more stable |
| Net-new site adds (restaurant + retail separately) | Organic growth signal — critical to separate the two |
| Software ARPU per site (blended) | Upsell / attach working? |
| Voyix Commerce Platform (VCP) enterprise contracts | New platform adoption — 21 cumulative through Q1 2026; ~5% penetration of ~400 enterprise customers |
| Hardware revenue ($M) | Intentionally declining (Ennoconn ODM from Q2 2026); reported revenue down 13-18% all of 2026 as a result |
| Adj. EBITDA ($M, margin %) | FCF guides to +40-62% YoY 2026 on pro-forma basis |
 
**Restaurant vertical — track specifically**:
 
VYX's restaurant segment competes with Toast and Shift4 Dine at the enterprise/chain level. NCR has deep legacy penetration in enterprise restaurant chains (QSR, fast casual), but has been losing ground. Track:
- **Net restaurant site adds per quarter** — has been negative (attrition). Any quarter with positive net adds is an inflection event.
- **Restaurant ARPU** — management doesn't disclose explicitly; back-calculate from segment revenue ÷ registered locations. Should be growing toward Toast's blended ARR/location as software attach improves.
- **VCP contract wins in restaurant vertical** — named or unnamed, these are proof the platform is competitive. Any named QSR or casual dining chain win is tier-1 signal.
- **Key logo wins / retention** — NCR has longstanding relationships with McDonald's franchisees, Subway, large QSR groups. Any disclosure of renewals on new VCP terms (vs. legacy terms) signals successful upgrade conversion.
**Inflection signals to watch**:
- RCV crossing $350M+ = VCP pipeline building; indicates 2027 ARR will accelerate
- Restaurant net-new sites turning positive = attrition has stopped; growth mode
- Restaurant ARPU expanding YoY = upsell/migration to VCP working
- Recurring % crossing 75%+ = hardware dilution has normalized
- Any named enterprise restaurant logo win = competitive credibility confirmed
- Hardware revenue declining faster than RCV growing = near-term headwind, structural positive
**Note**: NCR Atleos (NATL — the ATM/cash business) is a separate company post-split. If asked about ATM-related metrics, that is NATL, not VYX.

## PAR — PAR Technology
 
**Business model**: Enterprise restaurant technology platform — cloud POS (Brink), loyalty/CRM (Punchh), digital ordering (MENU), and back-office management (PAR Data Central). Unlike Toast (which leads with POS and moves upmarket), PAR almost always starts with enterprise chains and sells a multi-product platform. Heavily skewed toward QSR, fast casual, and national chains rather than indie/SMB restaurants.
 
**Primary metric**: **ARR** (Annual Recurring Revenue) — PAR reports ARR quarterly as its headline SaaS metric.
 
**Causal chain**:
`Brink POS locations × POS ARPU + Punchh locations × Punchh ARPU + MENU locations × MENU ARPU → Total ARR → Gross Profit → EBITDA`
 
PAR's revenue model is almost entirely subscription SaaS (no meaningful payments revenue yet — PAR Payment Services is early stage). This makes it a purer SaaS comp than Toast or Shift4.
 
**Key metrics to populate scorecard**:
 
| Metric | What it tells you |
|--------|-------------------|
| **ARR ($M, total)** | Primary SaaS health metric; guides to this explicitly |
| ARR YoY growth % | Is the enterprise land-and-expand motion working? |
| **Brink POS locations (live)** | Full POS deployment count — enterprise chain restaurants |
| **Brink net-new POS locations per quarter** | POS growth engine; enterprise deals = lumpy but high-value |
| **Punchh active locations** | Loyalty/CRM point-solution footprint; much larger than Brink (100K+ locations) |
| **Punchh-only vs. Brink+Punchh locations** | Cross-sell conversion rate — Punchh-only → full Brink POS is the highest-value motion |
| **MENU (digital ordering) active locations** | Digital ordering point-solution footprint |
| Platform attach (avg products per location) | Core thesis metric: PAR's value is multi-product; rising attach = deeper moat |
| Net Revenue Retention (NRR) | Measures upsell + churn net of expansions; target 110%+ |
| PAR Payment Services volume / locations | Nascent but high-margin if it scales; watch for any early disclosure |
| Subscription revenue ($M) | ARR converted to recognized revenue |
| Hardware revenue ($M) | Brink terminal hardware; low/zero margin, counts as location acquisition cost |
| Adj. EBITDA ($M, margin %) | PAR has been EBITDA-negative during growth investment; watch for path to profitability |
 
**POS vs. point-solution split — critical for PAR**:
 
PAR's total "location" count is dominated by Punchh loyalty customers, which is many multiples of its Brink POS count. This creates a large installed base for cross-sell but also inflates headline location metrics. Always separate:
- **Brink POS locations** (~40,000+ enterprise) — full platform, highest ARPU ($8–15K+ ARR/loc)
- **Punchh-only locations** (100,000+ total across Punchh) — loyalty SaaS only, lower ARPU (~$1–3K ARR/loc), but cross-sell opportunity
- **MENU-only locations** — digital ordering without POS; similar dynamic to Punchh-only
The number to focus on is Brink-to-Punchh cross-sell velocity: if PAR converts a meaningful portion of its 100K+ Punchh footprint into Brink POS customers, ARR could inflect significantly. Conversely, slow cross-sell suggests the products don't integrate cleanly enough to drive platform migration.
 
**Inflection signals to watch**:
- ARR accelerating above 20% YoY = enterprise motion gaining momentum
- Brink net-new POS locations accelerating = pipeline conversion working
- Platform attach rising (avg modules/location) = multi-product thesis validating
- NRR above 110% = net expansion in existing accounts > churn
- Any disclosure of Punchh → Brink conversion rate = cross-sell thesis
- PAR Payment Services first meaningful volume disclosure = new high-margin revenue leg beginning
- Any movement toward EBITDA profitability = growth investment maturing
**Key enterprise logos to track**: McDonald's (major PAR/Brink customer), Burger King franchisee groups, Dairy Queen, Subway, Taco Bell franchisees, large fast casual chains. PAR's enterprise chain relationships are its deepest competitive moat. Any disclosure of logo churn at a major chain is a high-priority negative signal.
 
**Competitive context**: Toast (POS — moving upmarket into chains), Oracle MICROS (legacy enterprise POS), Revel (mid-market), Qu/Onosys (QSR-focused), Olo (digital ordering overlap with MENU). PAR's differentiated position is the only independent multi-product platform purpose-built for enterprise chains — its biggest risk is Toast successfully moving upmarket and commoditizing the POS layer.

## TOST — Toast
 
**Business model**: Integrated POS + payments + SaaS for restaurants. Revenue = payment processing (% of GPV) + SaaS subscription + hardware (low margin, one-time). The most directly comparable restaurant tech company to both VYX (restaurant segment) and Shift4 Dine.
 
**Primary volume metric**: **Gross Payment Volume (GPV)** — total card payments processed through Toast terminals.
 
**Causal chain**:
`Net-new restaurant locations × avg GPV per location → total GPV × Fintech take rate → Fintech revenue`
`Locations × SaaS ARPU → ARR → SaaS gross profit`
 
**Key metrics to populate scorecard**:
 
| Metric | What it tells you |
|--------|-------------------|
| Gross Payment Volume (GPV, $B) | Volume throughput — aggregate output of the installed POS base |
| **Net-new POS locations (quarterly)** | Primary leading indicator; these are full POS deployments, not point-solution adds |
| **Total POS locations on platform** | Installed base; rate of growth = restaurant market share |
| **Point-solution locations (non-POS)** | Toast Tables, Toast Payroll, Toast Marketing, Toast Order & Pay used without full POS; lower ARPU, lower stickiness — watch to see if these convert to full POS |
| POS-to-total-location conversion rate | If disclosed; rising = platform land-and-expand working |
| **ARR ($M)** | Subscription software health; grows with locations × ARPU |
| **SaaS ARPU per POS location ($ annually)** | Module attach — $13–15K baseline; $18K+ = multi-product (payroll, scheduling, marketing, inventory) adoption |
| Platform locations (POS + ≥2 additional modules) | Highest-ARPU, most-retained cohort; if disclosed, track separately |
| Fintech revenue ($M) | Variable, GPV-linked; largest revenue line |
| Fintech gross profit ($M) and net take rate (bps) | After interchange; true payment economics — target 50+ bps net |
| Subscription gross profit ($M, margin %) | High-margin software layer; should expand as hardware mix declines |
| Hardware revenue ($M) | Near-zero or negative gross margin; customer acquisition cost in disguise |
| Non-restaurant / international POS locations | Expansion beyond U.S. restaurants |
| Key enterprise / chain logo wins | Named multi-unit chain (50+ locations) = move upmarket; note POS vs. point-solution product type |
 
**POS vs. point-solution locations — always split these**:
 
This distinction matters significantly across all restaurant tech companies. A "location" reported by Toast, PAR, Shift4, or VYX may mean very different things depending on whether it's a full POS deployment or a single point-solution module:
 
| Location type | Definition | ARPU profile | Stickiness |
|--------------|------------|--------------|------------|
| **POS location** | Restaurant running the company's full point-of-sale hardware + software | Highest (~$13–20K+ ARR) | Very high; POS is operationally embedded |
| **Platform location** | POS + ≥1 additional module (payroll, scheduling, loyalty, ordering) | 2–3x POS-only ARPU | Highest; multi-product = deep integration |
| **Point-solution only** | Single product without POS (e.g., loyalty-only, digital ordering only, back-office only) | Low (~$1–5K ARR) | Lower; easier to churn or swap |
 
When a company reports "locations on platform" or "live locations," always try to identify how many are POS deployments vs. point-solution deployments. A company that grows total location count primarily through point solutions (not POS) is generating lower-quality ARR. Conversely, a company that converts point-solution users into full POS customers is the highest-value motion.
 
**Restaurant-specific competitive benchmarks** — use to compare across TOST, FOUR, VYX, PAR:
 
| Metric | TOST | FOUR (Shift4 Dine) | VYX (restaurant) | PAR (Brink) |
|--------|------|-------------------|-----------------|-------------|
| Net-new **POS** locations/qtr | ~6,000–8,000 | ~2,000–4,000 (est.) | Negative → target flat | ~1,000–2,000 (enterprise) |
| Total **POS** locations | ~130,000+ | ~40,000+ (est.) | ~50,000–65,000 | ~40,000+ (chain/enterprise) |
| Point-solution-only locations | Limited (mostly POS-led) | Gateway-only merchants | Legacy hardware sites | Punchh-only + MENU-only (100K+ total) |
| Blended ARPU (POS) | ~$13–15K ARR | Higher (enterprise/hotel mix) | ~$8–12K ARR | ~$8–15K ARR (chain-skewed) |
| GPV per POS location/yr | ~$850K–$1M | Higher (hotel/stadium skew) | Lower (legacy pricing) | Not disclosed (enterprise chain focus) |
 
These benchmarks shift each quarter — update from each earnings print.
 
**Inflection signals to watch**:
- Net-new location add acceleration = fastest-growing restaurant tech platform; 6,000+ per quarter is healthy; 4,000 or below is decelerating
- SaaS ARPU crossing $15K+ annually = multi-module attach is working (payroll, scheduling, etc.)
- Fintech net take rate expansion = pricing power or better interchange mix
- Enterprise / chain wins (>50 locations per account) = successfully moving upmarket
- GPV per location holding or growing = same-store sales growth in the customer base
- International location count > 5% of total = geographic expansion becoming real
**Key logo wins to track**: Named QSR franchisee groups, regional fast casual chains, hotel F&B. Enterprise deals (franchisees with 50+ locations) are disproportionately meaningful because they imply future GPV at scale.

## FIS — Fidelity National Information Services
 
**Business model**: Enterprise software + services for banks and capital markets firms. **Important context**: FIS completed the separation of Worldpay (its merchant acquiring/payment processing business) in January 2024. Post-separation, FIS is a pure-play financial institution software company — it is no longer a direct payment processing competitor in the restaurant/merchant space. Worldpay is now independently owned (by GTCR/private equity) and operates separately.
 
FIS is relevant as a **broader payments infrastructure and bank tech competitor** — particularly for companies building bank-embedded commerce (digital banking, embedded payments, bank-issued cards used in restaurant transactions).
 
**Two segments post-Worldpay separation**:
 
**Banking Solutions** (~65% of revenue):
 
| Metric | What it tells you |
|--------|-------------------|
| Banking Solutions revenue ($M, YoY) | Core segment health |
| ARR ($M) and recurring revenue % | SaaS transformation progress |
| Revenue per bank client | ARPU expansion / cross-sell |
| Digital banking active users (if disclosed) | Consumer-facing engagement metric |
| New bank client signings (# and $) | New logo pipeline |
| Client retention rate (%) | Churn signal; large bank contracts are very sticky |
 
**Capital Markets Solutions** (~35% of revenue):
 
| Metric | What it tells you |
|--------|-------------------|
| Capital Markets revenue ($M, YoY) | Segment health |
| Recurring revenue % | Mix shift to SaaS |
| New client ACV | Deal pipeline signal |
 
**What to track in earnings**:
- Recurring revenue % (target: 70%+ of total)
- ARR growth (primary transformation metric)
- Adj. EBITDA margin and free cash flow conversion
- Debt reduction / capital allocation (FIS took on significant leverage for the Worldpay deal; watch leverage ratio)
- Any commentary on Worldpay relationship (residual revenue sharing agreements may exist)
**Competitive relevance to restaurant tech**: Indirect. FIS matters as the banking infrastructure underlying the payment rails that restaurant companies use — card issuance, core banking for restaurant operators, treasury management. FIS is not a direct restaurant POS or payments competitor post-Worldpay, but remains relevant as a potential acquirer of restaurant tech companies or as the banking partner behind embedded finance products (e.g., if Toast or PAR launches a business banking product, FIS may be the underlying bank tech provider).
 
**Key distinction from Fiserv (FISV)**: FIS and Fiserv are frequently confused. Fiserv still operates Clover (SMB POS + payments) which IS a direct restaurant POS competitor, and Fiserv's Carat platform IS a payment processing competitor in enterprise restaurants. When James references restaurant or merchant payments competition, Fiserv (FISV) is more directly relevant than FIS. Always clarify which company is being discussed.

## General Business Model Templates
 
Use these when analyzing companies not in the registry above.
 
### Marketplace / Platform
Primary metric: **GMV or GOV** → Take rate → Net revenue → Contribution margin
Watch: Take rate trend, AOV, buyer/seller frequency, geographic mix, new category penetration
 
### Payments Processor
Primary metric: **Payment volume** ($B) → Net revenue yield (bps) → EBITDA
Watch: Spread/yield compression, merchant count & attrition, vertical mix (higher-margin = better), integration depth (full-stack vs. gateway-only)
 
### POS / Location-Based SaaS
Primary metric: **Installed base (sites / locations)** → ARPU → ARR → EBITDA
Watch: Net-new sites, churn rate, ARPU expansion from upsell, hardware vs. software revenue mix, recurring % of total
 
### E-Commerce Enablement
Primary metric: **GMV processed** → Attach rate → Revenue → FCF
Watch: GMV vs. e-commerce category growth (share signal), attach rate trend, payments penetration, enterprise vs. SMB mix
 
### Delivery / On-Demand Marketplace
Primary metric: **Order volume or GOV** → Take rate + Ads → Revenue → Contribution profit per order
Watch: Order frequency per user, AOV, ad revenue / GOV, new category expansion, geographic expansion margin profile

## Quick Reference: What "Beat" Really Means by Business Type
 
| Business | Superficial beat | Real beat |
|----------|-----------------|-----------|
| Marketplace | Revenue beat | GOV growth + take rate stable or expanding |
| Payments | Revenue beat | Volume acceleration + spread stable |
| SaaS/POS | Revenue beat | ARR growth + net new sites + ARPU expansion |
| E-commerce platform | Revenue beat | GMV acceleration + attach rate improvement |
| Delivery | Revenue beat | GOV growth + contribution profit per order improvement |
 
When volume decelerates but revenue beats (because take rate expanded), flag the distinction explicitly — it often indicates monetization pressure ahead.
